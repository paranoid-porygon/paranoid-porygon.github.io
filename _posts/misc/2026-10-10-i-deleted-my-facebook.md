It's not news that [facebook][experiment] [sucks][disinfo] [shit][genocide] and in its current state (if not from the outset) provides nothing of value to the world and only serves to irradiate the brains of your boomer parents and GenX in-laws. It managed to ingrain itself within the internet ecosystem as a combination of federated login provider, Craigslist usurper, AIM replacement, and dystopian Eventbrite alternative, the combination of which and its near universal adoption by everyone you know for those purposes make not having an account a dicey proposition. But I found an exit strategy that works, one that is ostensibly simple but in reality requires prolonged effort: being extremely annoying about getting everyone you care to talk to to install Signal. 

<!--excerpt-->

I have had multiple Facebook accounts over the course of my life: several under variations of my real name, plus some burners, aliases, and personas. With adult hindsight, it's absolutely bonkers to me that some people have kept the same facebook accounts since they were minors, pictures and stupid posts included, and it's a testament to Facebook's desertion of bare minimum Trust & Safety policies that that was ever allowed. I even have former teachers that publicly posted pictures of their students while they were still in grade school, and even tagged them; it's freak shit and they should have known better.

Not everything needs to be shared with everyone, and the incentives intrinsic to widespread/public sharing are unhealthy. Additionally, based on my professional experience, having a Facebook account makes it significantly easier to stalk and dox a person; even if your account isn't public, your tech-inept family members might not have locked theirs down and still tag you or type out your full name (even if you remove the tag hyperlink), making you easy to identify.

The future of the social web is its past: small, self-governing communities and person-to-person instant messaging. You know, web forums and AIM/IRC. Facebook tacitly admitted this a few years ago when they claimed that Groups were their best feature and with their acquisition of WhatsApp. The popularity of Discord further demonstrates this reality. Social media platforms that are private, user-manageable, enable the use of pseudonyms, and free of ads are what people want and what people use; Signal is all of these.

Signal is a non-profit funded open source end-to-end encrypted instant messaging application that requires a cellular phone and number to use but has official desktop apps for Windows, MacOS, and Linux (and you can shoehorn it into running on BSD). The Signal team is about to introduce accounts that don't require a number, improving anonymity, but requiring payment to stave off misuse by scammers. The most private way to use it is to get a Phreeli eSim that you pay for with cash or a [privacy card](https://privacy.com) and use that number to register your Signal account, and then create a Signal alias to use instead of your cell number. That way, your Signal number cannot be reverse-looked up. There are even more private alternatives to Signal, but for this particular use case (keeping in touch with my friends and family), Signal is a reasonable tradeoff between convenience and privacy that isn't too onerous for people who can't be bothered to figure out PGP.

I retain a small handful of burner and alias accounts on Facebook, however creating new ones is proving to be increasingly difficult. I was not able to create an account from behind a VPN and had to tether to cellular internet, however I later accessed that account from behind a VPN and saw several of my account privileges frozen due to "unusual activity" and full privileges can only be reinstated if I--you guessed it!-- install the Facebook app to my phone and use my phone camera to scan my face. If that's a requirement for continued use, I'll just find an alternative. We can simply choose not to build the panslopticon.

Nobody has ever made the argument that social media is good for you. It feels good in the moment because it makes you *feel* more informed, but that informedness is specious. You are better off just subscribing to the Bluesky (so long as it remains ad-free) or mailing lists of your preferred news outlets; I've had a much better time with news in my inbox than I ever had with having to trudge through a Facebook feed past AI porn and ads to see the occasional sensationalized headline from a no-name website. You can even use Reddit as a mailinglist (or RSS feed, [for now][reddit-rss]) to send you the most pertinent updates from the subs you follow.

Speaking of RSS: just start a blog under a pseudonym. Any revealing life updates you share via Signal to the contacts that matter, and all other poasting can happen on the blog.

I don't plan on restarting my real-name Facebook account. I don't get anything out of having an account on there except being baited into responding to irritatingly stupid takes. And even having an account could be a potential roadblock for some professional roles I might pursue in the future. So this site and Signal are all you get.

I'll see you in hell, Zuck.
