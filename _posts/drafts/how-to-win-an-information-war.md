During one of Barnes & Noble's hardcover book sales, I discovered "How to Win an Information War: The propagandist who outwitted Hitler" by Peter Pomerantsev. It's a biography of British wartime counter-propagandist Sefton Delmer, but it's also an examination of Russian propaganda during the ongoing invasion of Ukraine and how it follows the same pattern as Nazi propaganda, and it's also somewhat of a manual on how to engage with people who fall prey to propaganda and some ideas as to how to counter it. While the author didn't make many explicit comparisons to American right-wing media (the book was published in March of 2024), there are obvious inferences meant to be drawn that can be useful in responding to Trump 2.0-era rhetoric.

One of the first and unfortunate conclusions that Allied counter-propagandists arrived at is that you cannot engage propagandized people in good faith. Trying to appeal to the better angels of their natures or attempting to reason with them by appealing to the truth historically does not work.
