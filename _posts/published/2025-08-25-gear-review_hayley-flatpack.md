A couple years ago, one of my Instagram mutuals posted that they were selling a limited edition set of both the Hayley Strategic D3CRM and Flatpack Plus in M81 Woodland camo. I purchased them with the expectation of building a full hike-and-shoot/competition/goofing around kit around them, entirely in M81 camo. Several months later, I finally did exactly that. This allowed me to test both the D3CRM and the Flatpack Plus, the latter of which is the focus of this review.


Here’s the verdict up front: **I honestly hate the Flatpack Plus and ended up selling it.** Not just “this isn’t right for me” or “it didn’t fit my particular use case”. I think the Flatpack Plus is absolutely terrible and doesn’t have any niche that it fills that couldn’t be filled by another, better designed, less expensive alternative. The only reason people buy these things is because their namesake, Mr. Travis “That wasn’t an N.D., I promise” Hayley, is a Jordan Peterson-type guru figure to fatherless Boog Bois and airsofters.


The Hayley Strategic Flatpack Plus is a rectangle with straps that looks like if you were trying to render a backpack on a Playstation One. According to their website, its intended use case is to “[bridge] the gap between a small assaulter pack and a full-sized day pack”. It has integrated molle straps for attaching to a plate carrier with webbing on the rear plate bag, and it additionally comes with backpack straps of dubious usefulness; more on that later. The page on Hayley’s site for the Flatpack Plus also lists numerous accessories that are compatible with the pack, which I suspect is the primary business model of these hypebeast tactical gear manufacturers: *Accessory Creep* (and to my knowledge, I am coining this term right here and right now, and you are free to use it under a Creative Commons license).


The Flatpack Plus has a small front pocket with elective accordion-style expansion, however I found the actual zippered opening of this pocket to be too small to fit things I would want quick access to, namely extra magazines or a poncho. 


There is a “middle” pocket behind that which is taller (longer?) but doesn’t have any expansion capability, and I found when I had the main pocket filled with gear, this middle pocket was unusable for anything thicker than a quarter of a ream of printer paper.


The main pocket, which does have accordion-style expansion, is designed to be opened like a clamshell. Loop velcro on the inside of the pack allows you to affix Hayley Strategic branded zipper compartments to contain your gear without it spilling on the ground when you try to access it (because that’s what otherwise happens with clamshell packs).


Finally, there is a hydration bladder pocket which was too shallow for my 3-liter Camel Bag bladder (which in all honesty is probably less a flaw of the Flatpack Plus than it is a mistake on my part for owning a hydration bladder that’s bigger than the average person needs for a <24 hour outing). I instead ended up shoving two pieces of cardboard into this pocket to give the Flatpack Plus some semblance of rigidity/comfort.


Speaking of comfort, the Flatpack Plus comes with what are quite possibly the worst shoulder straps ever sold with a backpack; if you look up almost any Reddit or YouTube review of this pack, that is a universal complaint. Having purchased it secondhand, mine didn’t come with the shoulder straps and I was spared this indignity. I ended up purchasing some aftermarket PackStraps in M81 camo as a replacement and they actually worked pretty decently.


Here’s what drives me insane about the Flatpack Plus: this pack is ostensibly meant to be worn affixed to a plate carrier via molle on the rear plate bag. But if that were the case, why is the pack designed in such a way that you have to splay it on the ground to access its contents? Even if the argument is that you need a buddy/squadmate/life partner to access the pack which is semi-permanently affixed to your rear plate bag, they only have easy access to the small front pouch with the unfortunately small opening or the middle pouch which is effectively useless. If they try to open the main pouch where most of your gear is probably stored, they’ll have to clamshell it all the way open to get access, dangling half the pack awkwardly across your ass and spilling all over the ground anything that wasn’t zipped up in a Hayley Strategic Clear Top Insert Bag and velcroed to the inside of the pack. This clearly isn’t meant to be a plate carrier pack.


I know that there’s an AXL aftermarket accessory that allows you to attach the Flatpack Plus to swift clips so you can partially detach it from the rear plate bag and swing it around in front of you to access your kit, but at that point you are just reinventing the wheel in a needlessly complicated way at an added expense when it would be much simpler to wear it using backpack straps that you can don and doff like a reasonable human being with a normal fucking backpack. But then the backpack straps that the Flatpack Plus comes with are incredibly thin and uncomfortable, so even then you still have to buy some sort of aftermarket accessory to make it not suck.


I should probably mention that Hayley also sells (re: Accessory Creep) different straps that can be used to attach this pack using swift clips to a micro chest rig, such as their own D3CRM, as a sort of replacement harness. While I did not buy these straps, I do own a Wisemen Company HX Harness that has molle on the back of the harness for attaching hydration packs/backpacks, which was exactly what I tried with the Flatpack Plus’s integrated molle straps; that ended up going exactly as poorly as I anticipated for all the reasons outlined above, so I promptly reverted to using it with the shoulder straps.


Every time I used the Flatpack Plus, I kept finding that I wished it had actual purpose-made or at least bigger-mouthed pockets or external molle webbing. I don’t know if Hayley’s goal was a “minimalist” pack when they designed this thing, but it felt less “high speed, low drag” and more just plain limiting.


I’m not even going to waste time talking about build quality because frankly the overall dumb design of this thing makes it not really even worth consideration. I strongly recommend literally any other ~15-liter day pack from an actual backpack company. Additionally, there are better options for pouches that affix to your rear plate bag. Hell, you’d be better off purchasing a patent-infringing Chinese copy of the Flatpack because at least that’ll be more reasonably priced and might make a serviceable snack and hydration bladder pack. I ultimately replaced mined with a beavertail-style that actually has useful dedicated pockets and opens from the top.


The Flatpack Plus is incredibly overpriced for what it is. $180 for the non-limited edition version gets you a pack with no external molle attachment points, non-existent rigidity, dogshit-tier shoulder straps, and frankly goofy and borderline useless pocket arrangement and design. It makes for a poor backpack unless you attach it to a plate carrier, for which there isn’t even a good argument; as a civvy, you will likely need quick access to your own kit, and I've even heard of active duty folks getting razzed by their peers for having to rely on other people to get gear for them from their backpack. The Flatpack Plus is yet another piece of evidence that I present as to why you should be extremely skeptical if not outright avoidant of any piece of gear being advertised by and making the rounds among Leviathan Group influencers.
