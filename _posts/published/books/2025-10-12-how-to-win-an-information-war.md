During one of Barnes & Noble's hardcover book sales, I discovered ["How to Win an Information War: The propagandist who outwitted Hitler" by Peter Pomerantsev](https://snfagora.jhu.edu/resources/how-to-win-an-information-war/). It's a biography of British wartime counter-propagandist Sefton Delmer, but it's also an examination of Russian propaganda during the ongoing invasion of Ukraine and how it follows the same pattern as Nazi propaganda, and it's also somewhat of a manual on how to engage with people who fall prey to propaganda and some ideas as to how to counter it. While the author didn't make many explicit comparisons to American right-wing media (the book was published in March of 2024), there are obvious inferences meant to be drawn that can be useful in responding to Trump 2.0-era rhetoric.

One of the first and unfortunate conclusions that Allied counter-propagandists arrived at is that you cannot engage propagandized people in good faith. Trying to appeal to the better angels of their natures or attempting to reason with them by appealing to the truth historically does not work. Above all, they are looking to be validated via a sense of belonging, and propaganda gives them exactly that. Extricating them from their "found family" is not easy, so you need to give them a new one.

Another observation was that people living in a heavily propagandized regime that are sympathetic to opposition will consume media that opposes the regime if it gives them plausible deniability if challenged on their media consumption. In the case of making counter-propaganda targeted at Germans in WWII, this looked like creating a fake German language news service that would often scoop news faster than the real Nazi outlets; this lent credibility to the counter-propaganda radio station and permitted Germans to listen in with less risk to themselves if caught. This appearance of credibility would often be knowingly abused to inspire Germans to engage in behavior that undermined the Nazi regime; one hiliarious example of this was convincing Germans to send jars of their piss by mail to Berlin for inspection.

## Quotes

Some interesting quotes from the book:

Page 195:

> Since the middle of the war, Henry Dicks, a psychiatrist working for [British] Military Intelligence, had been exploring the mindsets of German soliders... He conducted and reviewed more than six hundred interviews with POWs and concluded that "the psychology of Nazis, as disclosed by detailed individual investigation of large numbers of them and subsequent statistical validation, fails to show gross mental disorder. It is characterised by an unconscious over-emphasis of paternal authority, evasion of responsibility by blaming scape-goats, over-valuation of masculinity, and depreciation of feminine and tender influences."

Page 197:

> Although some Germans were a psychologically ideal fit for Nazi propaganda, not all were. [Dicks] calculated that 10 percent of German soldiers were "fanatical" Nazis and a further 25 percent were "believers with reservations." As the war neared its end, Dicks was most worried about this 25 percent: their Nazism was mixed with a more general admiration of German militarism, and they could easily blend in as good patriots in the Germany of the future without changing their underlying authoritarian predilections.
> The largest group of German soldiers was the 40 percent of "unpolitical men." They were largely indifferent to the type of regime as long as it gave them "order and security." "Passive anti-Nazis" made up 15 percent; 10 percent were active anti-Nazis."

Page 215:

> The legend of "good" German soldiers who defied or were at least ideologically opposed to the Nazis, a legend [British propagandist] Delmer had done so much to ferment, was now being claimed as having been a reality, and it was enabling former senior Nazi officers to ascend to the commanding heights of the West German government, security services, and industry.

Page 216:

> "...all the peoples of Western Europe have developed the habit of blaming their misfortunes on some force out of their reach." [Philospher Hannah] Arendt tied the lack of responsibility to the Nazis' relativist attitude to the truth: if you thought facts and truth were all "subjective," then everyone could choose their own reality and avoid responsibility:
> > In all fields there is a kind of gentlemen's agreement by which everyone has a right to his ignorance under the pretext that everyone has a right to his opinion... The average German honestly believes this free-for-all, this nihilistic relativity about facts, to be the essence of democracy. In fact, of course, it is a legacy of the Nazi regime.
> Arendt thought the very ease with which Germans had surrendered Nazi ideology without any serious debate about its precepts was itself part of Nazism: "What one is up against is not indoctrination but the incapacity or unwillingness to distinguish altogether between fact and opinion."

Page 222:

> [During the ongoing war in Ukraine,] when Ukrainian activists tried mass telephone calls to Russians [to undermine support for the invasion], they found that some 80 percent would hang up during the first twenty seconds if the calls were about the devastation caused by the war, but only 30 percent hung up when the call focused on theier personal interests, such as a special tax they had to pay to support Russia's newly occupied lands. When the call was about help to Russian veterans, 43 percent hung up, but only 25 percent hung up when the call was about travel limitations caused by the war.

Page 231:

> In the immediate postwar years the West German press had accused Delmer of being a determined *Deutschlandhasser* (German hater) for seeing Nazism returning. By the time [his] memoirs came out, that criticism of Delmer had grown milder as Germans began to analyze the truth about the continued presence of senior Nazis in their system. Delmer's worries that the worst of the past would return also softened with time as he saw a new generation of Germans confront the Nazi legacy.

## Final World

I recommend this book. It's a biography of an obscure but important figure and organization that led to the Allied victory in WWII Europe. It additionally outlines common pitfalls people make in trying to reach propaganda victims and demonstrates possible alternative means of persuasion. It convinced me to stop trying to reason with Trumpists by appealing to reason but instead make arguments on the basis of Christian principles to slowly chip away at their support.
