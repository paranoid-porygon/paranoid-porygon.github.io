# Books

I'm finishing "Rebellion: How Antiliberalism is Tearing America Apart...Again" by Robert Kagan right now, and it's a super brief but comprehensive history and treatise on how anti-liberalism has been here since the founding of the nation and isn't strictly the consequence of technology, material conditions, military conflict, or the combination thereof, but rather is inherently ideological and has persisted across the southern colonies to the secessionists to the America First movement to the John Birchers to the Neoliberals to the Neoconservatives and finally with Trumpers, and that it is the greatest threat to the American project and how the [then potential, now actual] results of the 2024 election could finally end American liberal democracy. It's to be determined how accurate that prognosticating ends up being, but the history covered in this book makes it worth reading regardless.

I finished reading "The Nazi Hunters" by Andrew Nagorski, a very accessible overview of the various efforts by the Allied governments as well as private individuals who worked to bring war criminals and perpetrators of the Holocaust to justice after the war, starting with the Nuremburg Trials and ending with the sentencing of nonagenarian Nazis in the 2010s. One anecdote in the book stood out to me: less than 20 years after the end of the war, by which point the Holocaust was well-known and documented and every German was aware of it, young Germans were skeptical or even incredulous as to the existence of Anne Frank and the veracity of her published diary. Much like how post-Civil War memory tried to at best rehabilitate or relitigate and at worst outright deny the causes of secession and the war, post-WWII Germans wanted to move past their evil past to the point of erasure of their history. The Nazi hunters forced them to face their history.

I'm currently finishing "Idoru" by William Gibson. I'm liking it more than "Virtual Light" and about as much (maybe a little more) than "Neuromancer". The job and skills of the main character are eerily similar to my and my old team's threat investigation and web sleuthing work. If nothing else, the novel made me miss that job.

I had started "Meme Wars" but paused reading it, partly because I felt like I already knew most of what it had to say, partly because there were other books I felt were higher priority ("Rebellion"), and partly because I didn't care for how the authors wrote in the first chapter; the book is supposed to be an accessible, popular history of how meme culture and online forums have bled into real-world politics, however it appears to be written for an audience that already has a decent amount of familiarity with online spaces, lingo, and events, which makes this less useful for brining my boomer mother up-to-speed about Anonymous, GamerGate, 8chan, and the Proud Boys.

My friends spun up a server and we had a blast with it, even if I sunk considerably less time in it than they did.

Until I make a decision, I'm going to play the original STALKER trilogy.

# Hobbies

I mostly played the Pokemon TCG rather than video games this year. I got the chance to go to NAIC, mostly as an excuse to visit New Orleans again. I performed terribly but still had fun just hitting as many local restaurants as I could. Didn't interact with much of the PTCG community while I was there but frankly feel like a bit of a fish-out-of-water: I heavily resent how the Pokemon Company preys on people, especially children, with ADHD and gambling "addictions" so it's difficult to interact with people that while ostensibly being anti-capitalist blow their earnings on sealed product and cross-country trips. Going forward, I only plan to purchase singles and complete off-the-shelf decks for myself and only participate in major tournaments in my geographical region or where I have extended family/other excuses to visit. I also plan to pivot to playing retro formats in earnest, particularly Base-Neo and RS-PK, in favor of standard.

I didn't do nearly as much competitive shooting this year as previous years, partially due to an active job hunt and financial disruption but mostly because my local range where I did monthly matches shut down. I let my gun club membership lapse as I didn't think I was getting my money's worth. I plan to slim down my firearms and paraphernalia to bare essentials (carry gun, deep concealment gun, home defense PCC, sporting/SHTF rifle) and downgrade this to a tier-2 hobby. I did get the opportunity to test my 13/7" rifle at 600 yards and it performed as well as I hoped, so I'm likely to lock in on that barrel length.
