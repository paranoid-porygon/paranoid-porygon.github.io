Here are my stray thoughts at this moment regarding the ~~Blue Wave~~ ~~Bluenami~~ [Blue Third Impact](https://youtu.be/_N66Hud_Tq4) of an election last night.

## It's the economy, you fucking dumbass

Americans voted for Trump in 2024 because inflation wasn't recovering fast enough for them; he won by a [relatively slim margin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election#Results) with less than 50% of the vote. It was hardly a mandate or indicator of a culture-wide repudiation of "woke", despite many far-right conservatives celebrating the return of [being allowed to be impolite at the office](https://nymag.com/intelligencer/article/inauguration-trump-supporters-conservative-movement-post-maga.html).

Last night proved that it really just is the economy, stupid. That's what voters care about. All the ads focusing on trans people and roving Latino mobs backfired spectacularly because no normal person cares about that shit.

It didn't help that the proliferation of ICE death squads, [unelected](https://youtu.be/CrPO9QXH8oM) Flock/Axon surveillance, and AI gobbling up the majority of the US economy coincided with Trump's second term. Even if none of those were strictly his doing (the ICE one definitely is), Americans likely associate his administration with the devolution of the US into a Gibsonian dystopia.

## The role of money in politics is (probably) overstated

Elon Musk [blew $400M via his super PAC](https://www.msn.com/en-us/money/markets/elon-musk-s-record-election-bet-backfired-the-backlash-may-reach-beyond-one-billionaire/ar-AA2gJy4g) on candidates that would be sympathetic to his business and Great Replacement interests, and [over $.99 per $1 he spent went to a losing candidate](https://www.wired.com/story/voters-set-dollar400-million-of-elon-musks-money-on-fire/). The same was true of pro-data center industry spending and their preferred candidates. Dems were the first to latch onto the anti-data center and AI-skepticism movement, especially with Trump becoming an extremely loud ~~AI~~ SI booster.

If the world's only trillionaire and the largest commercial interests in the global economy right now couldn't prevent what happened last night, it brings into question how meaningfully influential donations, lobbying, and bribery really are. I still believe on principle that Citizens United needs to be reversed, but we need to be realistic about what impact if any that would have on voter preference.

I remain more concerned about billionaires and corporate interests buying up legacy and social media outlets in an attempt to control discourse and disseminate propaganda/memes. This is a **major** issue that Dems need to acknowledge and address swiftly if they get a tirfecta in 2028. After climate change, it might be **the** issue to end all issues.

## Fox is very close to irrelevant

Fox News is consumed almost exclusively by a demographic that is about to die off. Younger conservatives are often far more viscerally bigoted than their predecessors, but terrestrial Fox News can't be as openly racist as online personalities can. That said, young male voters were roughly 50/50 split last night and might not be as dyed-in-the-wool fascist as many lefties have pontificated; like most voters, they ultimately care about their material concerns (not being drafted to fight a war in Iran, gas being too expensive, they can't buy RAM to play Fortnite because of AI, an abysmal job market for recent grads, etc).

## Our presidential system is plainly obviously flawed and needs to be replaced

There is no way to read what happened last night as anything other than a resounding repudiation of Trump, if not Republicans and Conservatism broadly. Not a single seat flipped red. Multiple southern states elected Blue governors for the first time in decades. There is obvious, widespread discontent with the Trump administration's method of governance.

And yet, he remains president for two more years.

Were this a parliamentary system, he would be replaced in short order. Or even if we just had a "vote of no confidence" on our biennial ballots. 

But we are stuck with a lame duck with veto powers and the ability to wage unnecessary wars for two more years, despite a clear mandate from the electorate to knock it off. We are going to see him lash out at Americans rather than behave as a public servant going foward, and Republicans who have already started to distance themselves from Trump are going to feel that they can do so even more openly now.
