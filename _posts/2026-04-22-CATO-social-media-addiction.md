CATO once again has [a half-baked take][CATO-social-media] resulting from motivated reasoning (their motivation being that "government is always bad and should be minimized if not eliminated because it'll otherwise spend money") rather than letting the facts speak for themselves; this is clear by the end of the article where the author only writes 3 paragraphs about the allegedly poor research on the topic of "social media addiction" and 6 paragraphs on why readers shouldn't trust "diagnostic authorities" because it'd lead to government money getting spent on the problem.

One positive thing I will say is that the author's distinction between dependence, habit, and addiction are spot-on. I have made this exact same distinction in arguments about whether or not "sex addiction" is real (it isn't, doesn't fit the clinical definition of addiction, and that's why the DSM doesn't recognize sex addiction as a legitimate condition). However every time I made that distinction it was with the recognition that 1) I'm splitting hairs because a chronic bad habit functionally looks like addiction and treatment for it might look like what treatment looks like for an addict, and 2) this is especially true for people with any executive function disorders (ADHD, ASD) or compulsory disorders.

CATO, this author, and libertarianism more broadly suffer from their foundational theory that people are all completely rational agents that exist as pure souls inhabiting biological machines, that the soul has complete control over the brain, and it is never held hostage by a malfunctioning brain that might have behavioral disorders. We know this is bogus, and the existence of executive dysfunction and compulsory disorders cast serious aspersions on this idea of perfect agency. People with those disorders and addictions *need* external prodding at the least (i.e. interventions) if not institutionalization to overcome them. I don't see a case for institutionalizing someone for their "social media addiction", but I also think it's naive not to call a spade a spade in the face of 14 years of evidence (see ["The Anxious Generation"][ag] and ["Careless People"][cp]) that social media companies knowingly create content delivery systems that, in their words, function like a [slot machine][sm] (the CATO author acknowledges that gambling is an addiction), and that these feeds affect children whose brains are still developing and people with executive dysfunction and compulsive disorders much more severely.

CATO writers always make the implicit argument that you shouldn't expect the government to save you, and that's because CATO don't want the government to do good things. I'm also going to tell you that you shouldn't expect the government to save you, but that's because you don't have time to wait for the government to go after social media companies. If you have kids, you can intervene **right now** to mitigate social media (that includes YouTube and TikTok!) addiction. If you chronically bedrot and doomscroll and you have a partner that can intervene, have them do so; if you don't, create impediments during a moment of clarity to your ease of social media usage (uninstall the apps from your phone and just access them through desktop web browser, for instance).
