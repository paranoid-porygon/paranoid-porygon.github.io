I'm trying something new with this review and instead of writing a retrospective, comprehensive review once I finished it, I instead plan to write my thoughts as I read. As such, **this will be a fairly spoiler-laden review**. 

## two chapters in

I am skeptical that a kid as young as Brooks growing up with a right-wing guardian would have ended up as involved in local politics by high school graduation as he is. This almost feels like Doctorow is inventing a type of guy.

In fact, a lot of what has gone on in the worldbuilding up to this point can be described as "fantasy wish-fulfillment for Californians": guns are illegal everywhere, children are engaged in local politics, DHS still exists however its primary purpose is to hunt down white nationalists, the cops are white liberals or genderqueer brown people, antitrust finally came for Twitter and Google. This type of setting I would say fits pretty squarely into Cyberprep, as opposed to post-Cyberpunk, because of its optimism that we'll get things right soon rather than having to recover from a dystopia. I don't dislike this kind of thing on principle, but it really does just read like a wishlist of things that liberals and social democrats (of which I am one) want, and its kind of irksome because of it. I saw another reviewer describe this as "masturbatory" writing, and I'm inclined to agree.

It's also funny to me that "40 boxes of ammo" is made to sound like a frightening number. That's 800 rounds, which is maybe enough for a year's worth of typical range time if you go like every other month. Just a very Californian/Canadian thing to write.

## actually ya know what, DNF

I started reading [several][1] [reviews][2] for this book from folks who finished it, and they echoed my concerns and confirmed my fears that it doesn't get much better. I'm checking out only two chapters in, which is supremely disappointing.

I like a lot of Cory Doctorow's live talks and blog posts and I generally agree with him on how to make the internet, and the world more broadly, a better place. Unfortunately, I think the world would be an even *better* place if he were a better novelist. The Lost Cause is preachy right at the outset and did not appear to let up. Rather than waste my time, I stopped reading after the second chapter.

I am now reading Count Zero by William Gibson, a known quantity who I expect to act as a pallete cleanser. Even if Gibson acts like a #Resistance shitlib poaster online, at least he can write a good sci-fi novel. Literally the opposite of Cory Doctorow.
