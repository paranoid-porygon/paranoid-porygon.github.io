Both the United States and the USSR desperately wanted the world to view post-1917 global politics and conflict through a very simple dichotomy of capitalism vs. totalitariansim or imperialism vs communism (depending on which camp wrote your social studies curriculum). This would make it easier to get people to subscribe to Devil Theory (that the opposite camp is "the devil", ontologically evil, and therefore more attractive to dissidents who can act as patsies once persuaded of the evils of their home camp). 

Any events, evidence, witnesses, or theorists that tried to demonstrate otherwise were ignored, maligned, or silenced. This was the case during the Spanish Civil War, where Stalin-aligned communists tried to suppress information coming out of Spain about the true nature of the infighting and the democratic West oversimplified the events as simply a fight between anti-fascists and Francoists.

Orwell's experiences in Spain both inspired his lifelong commitment to democratic socialism and hardened his heart against Stalinism and its adjacent strains of communism. But he only came to these convictions after having fought *in a communist militia against fascists*. Nineteen Eighty-Four was not at all a broadly anti-socialist screed but rather a treatise [against totalitarianism][1] in all its forms (fascist, Francoist, Stalinist, Maoist, or any of their later descendants) regardless of their superficial left-right alignment. Orwell repeatedly referred to the Stalin-backed Spanish communists and their coalition members as "right-wing" because of their reversal of the anarchist and socialist revolutionary progress made in Spain during earlier parts of the civil war.

If you read Homage to Catalonia, I recommend reading the chapters/appendices in their original published order. The two appendices are absolutely essential to understand the events of the narrative chapters and elucidate Orwell's point of view.
