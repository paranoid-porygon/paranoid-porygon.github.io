Last weekend I was supposed to attend a prerelease event for a new Pokemon TCG set with a longtime friend of mine, but due to my bad habit of overcommiting my free time and double-booking I arrived late and didn't get to participate. Partially due to a notoriously stale standard format and partially due to an even more notorious scalper problem, prereleases are the only way that we engage with the PTCG anymore because they offer an even playing field and relative affordability. While I waited for my friend to finish the pre-release tournament (he went 3-0; please clap), I meandered around the small TCG store (they don't sell any other kinds of games), noticing that their bread-and-butter seemed to be the Pokemon TCG and that they didn't stock any Magic: The Gathering products at all. They had a couple of other niche TCGs' products for sale, almost none of which seemed remotely interesting to me, a grown-ass adult who doesn't have the patience to learn complex mechanics, time to participate in a highly-demanding competitive circuit, or desire to spend money on any of the games with card art made for anime-obsessed pedophiles. The only standouts were a pair of starter decks for the Cyberpunk&trade; TCG.

## State of the TCG Union

I have played the Pokemon TCG on-and-off since 1999. I have never been seriously competitive at it, but I enjoyed it for its simple (compared to Magic: the Gathering) rules, affordable staple cards, and characters that I know. 

But let's call a spade a spade: the Pokemon TCG is gambling that targets children and people on the Autism/ADHD spectrum. Scalpers also recognized this and respond to those perverse incentives, hoarding Pokemon TCG "product" to create additional, artificial scarcity in the hopes that desperate consumers would pay their inflated prices. This has led to an ongoing --albeit one-sided-- tug-of-war battle between the most profitable franchise to have ever existed ostensibly trying to keep "product" more available and affordable to non-scalper consumers, and a bunch of unemployable cynics who believe they are [entitled to card scarcity][scalper-crashouts] to try to buy and HODL "product" faster than the aforementioned franchise can print it. A megacorp producing more plastic and paper waste to make an easy buck by exploiting people with poor-to-nonexistent impulse control and berry collecting instincts versus a bunch of rent-seeking layabouts cosplaying as business owners and entrepreneurs. You have my permission to hate everyone involved.

(If The Pokemon Company International were actually serious about beating scalpers, they would a) offer direct sale of minimum rarity cards for competitive play, or b) convert the game from a trading card game to a complete card game --like the semi-defunct Netrunner-- but TPCi will never do that because they make too much goddamn money from the scalper epidemic.) 

All of this is besides the fact that a single deck archetype has dominated the competitive metagame for almost 3 years straight, suriving two rotations and achieving "Tier 0" status (a.k.a. this is a stale format). There's no creativity or theorycrafting to be exercised here; just build Dragapult and memorize your lines and outs, especially for the mirror match. You might as well just play chess (actually, you should probably just play chess). 

I am told that Magic has its own set of problems (availability of cards, introduction of cynical cash-grab sets based around popular IPs that both break the game's lore and turn it into a shitty Super Smash Bros, complexity of gimmick mechanics introduced with each set, etc.) for which I am glad that I cashed out 6 years ago but have become fatiguing for those who were up-until-recently still invested in it. All of which is to say that the big two trading card games have fallen off, leaving room for niche and indie TCGs to try to steal some of their market. 

The Cyberpunk&trade; TCG is the first of these niche successors to catch my attention.

## Chippin' In 

I snagged the starter deck with the player character from 2077 on it, and my friend grabbed the other. We spoke to staff at the card shop and they revealed that the game has technically not launched yet and that these were "beta" set decks, that the game already had an active local scene, and that the game was pretty good. We both went home and started reading up on the rules and promptly registered for a prerelease tournament on the following Friday.

That prerelease has come and gone, and I managed to actually show up in time to participate this go-around.

I won't recite the rules of the game; those are easy enough to find elsewhere online and, thankfully, are not particularly complicated. I will give my initial impressions, which are positive, and share some more specific thoughts as to what sets this game apart and why it improves upon its competitors.

### What we liked

Games are comparatively shorter than Pokemon or Magic games, and this is by design. I'm not sure if this was because the designers wanted it to be a "play during your lunch break"-style game or because they wanted tournaments to be run quickly and smoothly, but I foresee it achieving both.

We could not find a single card that instructed players to "shuffle your deck". We both considered this a good thing if for no other reason than it prevents slowplay/delay of game during structured events like tournaments. It's much easier to get 3 games into a 50-minute round if you aren't constantly shuffling or prize checking or searching your deck for a 1-of card.

The Legends mechanic feels like it borrows from both Pokemon's prize system and Magic's commander variant, and fixes issues with both (the former sometimes breaking decks running several 1-of's which has led to [much discourse][prize-checking] about prize checking or alternative means of selecting prizes, and the latter of which being an entirely casual format without feasibility as a competitive game mode).

*I* feel that the choice not to have a dedicated resource card --think energy or lands-- but rather force the player to make Sophie's Choices about what playable cards to convert to resources is a fun way to force the player to make snap "microdecisions", and it removes dead weight cards. This is probably what allowed the designers of the Cyberpunk&trade; TCG to shrink the deck size to 40 cards and playsets to 3 cards. This feels like a game made specifically for people with small hands or who like to double- or triple-sleeve their cards, for which I thank them.

The next set --the Edgerunners Season 1 set-- is slated to launch in March of 2027, 5 months after the Welcome to Night City base set. That is almost triple the time between Pokemon TCG sets, affording more time to finish a collection (if you're a freak who's into that sort of thing) before the next set launches, or for players to "solve" or at least have time to play in the current format before a new one demands updating the metagame; I facetiously referred to this to my friend as an "adult release schedule" because it makes it easier for people older than the age of 23 with other hobbies, responsibilities, or a family to maybe keep up with the current metagame and get good enough at the game using what free time they can find before the formate changes.

Something I noticed and appreciated was that despite the game being labeled for ages 17+, and especially juxtaposed to all the anime-associated TCGs on the market, is how not-horny the game is. I don't think I saw one piece of card art that you could reasonably argue sexualizes and objectifies the character being depicted, regardless of gender. It might not be a game that you can get your mother to try, but she at least won't assume that you're a gooner incel if she glances at your decklist.

### Stray thoughts

It was a little disappointing that at this "prerelease" that I and my friend were the only two people who hadn't already played the game and weren't up on the metagame and all the matchups. I can't blame the publishers: the game was a Kickstarter project which had Alpha and Beta releases and playtesting, both necessary to build hype and test the game mechanics. But it felt like the players were all superfans; some of which I spoke to had not played any of the Big Three TCGs (Magic, Pokemon, or YuGiOh). This might not be a game for casuals, even if the mechanics lend themselves to casual play.

There is only so much recent Cyberpunk&trade; content that the publisher can mine for more card sets (Cyberpunk 2077, the DLC, and two seasons of Edgerunners) that the average person is going to be familiar with before they start having to reference more esoteric sources (comics, current and previous TTRPG editions), which even if they did wouldn't give them more than maybe 6 sets' worth of source material. If the game not only remains popular but grows in popularity, it will need more than 6 total sets, which leads to my theory/expectation that the Cyberpunk&trade; TCG will start mining other lowercase-c cyberpunk properties for new sets. The recent Ghost in the Shell adaptataion, the upcoming Neuromancer live action show, and yet another Blade Runner installment are all current or upcoming cyberpunk properties that are fresh enough to maybe justify inclusion into a broader cyberpunk TCG. We'll see if that materializes or if the game fizzles out before something like that becomes necessary.

## Verdict

{% include img.html alt_text="blue-blue-green control deck legends: Alt, Hanako, and Jackie" asset_path="" %}

I and my gaming buddy both felt that the game is well designed and reasonably priced (we split a booster box that came out to $5 a pack). Singles at this time are dirt-ass cheap on TCG Player, so you can build a competitive deck for less than $50. We intend to keep playing it, even if only casually, but honestly I like the idea of going back to a local-only competitive scene rather than a every local tournament feeling like a qualifier for the international circuit (which is what Pokemon does) and every competitor being the sweatiest dude you've met in your life, figuratively and often literally. 

The game is fun, and I hope that, like Johnny Silverhand, it [Never Fades Away][https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AN1RJF55NXI].
