The three correct barrel lengths for the AR-15 chabered in 5.56x45mm are 11.5", 14.5" and 18". You choose these barrel lengths based on your intended use case for the rifle/"pistol" and your likelihood of purchasing a suppressor, and you must choose the longest available standard gas system length for your chosen barrel. I will tolerate a 12.5" in the same way that I tolerate someone who claims to be a 1.5 on the Kinsey scale but only so long as you use mid-length gas (and acknowledge that you would let Lee Pace make love to you if the opportunity ever presented itself). If you try to make a case for 20", I will go out of my way to learn Vietnamese and use it to respond to your grandpa opinion.

## 14.5 inches

The 14.5" is the "general purpose" length. Provided you have a pinned and welded muzzle device that gets the legal length up to the non-SBR required minimum of 16", you have the shortest stocked rifle possible without having to file an ATF Form 1 (not counting 11.5" barrles that you figured out how to weld a suppressor to). This is the length of the M4 and is tried and tested. And it's not too long that a short K suppressor becomes too unwieldly at the end, should you opt to suppress it. Alternatively, you could just not suppress it and put a flash hider on the end and be good-to-go.

14.5" is great for competition, medium-range shooting (100-300 yards), practical home defense (i.e. where you point the rifle at the bedroom door while your partner calls 911 and checks the cameras), and defending the homeland after the outbreak of a third war with the British. It's perfectly cromulent for clearing a building if you have children, pets, or a mission critical server rack that sleep in a separate room from yours, and during a break-in you need to make sure that nobody is stealing your children, eating your pets, or uploading Conficker to your Active Directory server. And it can reach out to 600+ yards with some practice and the correct ammo; more on this later.

## 18 inches

If you never plan to suppress your rifle or if your main use case is shooting at the range or in competition, this is your barrel length. It is the shortest barrel that can reliably make use of the rifle-length gas system; before you interject and remind me of the 16" dissipator, I will remind *you* that the 14.5" barrel is already on this list and has negligibly different performance from a 16" whereas the 18" offers a noticeable step up in capability from the 14.5" as well as more reliability than the dissipator.

Did I mention how soft-shooting a rifle-length 18" AR-15 is? There's a reason why this barrel and gas length combo dominates the competition scene: it is so much easier to tame the recoil on this than any other length.

You also get hella effective distance out of this thing. 18" was the length that the Army chose for their Special Purpose Receiver (SPR), which is basically a much more convenient implementation of a Dedicated Marksman Rifle (DMR). Calibrate your gas, buffer, and optic for some heavy bullets and you will have the most fun day at the range out of anyone there.

I used to have an AK chambered in 5.56 (don't ask), and it had an 18" barrel. It was way more precise than it deserved to be, and it made me seriously consider making my one-and-done AR an 18"-er. I ultimately went with a 14.5" because I planned to suppress it, but the spreadsheet where I priced out an 18" build still calls to me like the One Ring called out to Bilbo.

## 11.5 inches

There's a reason why almost every SWAT team, FBI Hostage Rescue Team, Counter Assault Team, and spooky SOF team stopped using 9mm subguns and moved to very short ARs: better ballistics, more energy dumped into a target at practical distances, and reduce likelihood of overpenetration (again, with the caveat of correct ammo choice). There is a very, very compelling argument that 11.5" is the most practical barrel length for most civilian gun owners seeing as most of those claim to have purchased an AR for self-defense (for which 11.5" is ideal) but in reality usually just take it to shoot at a 25 yard indoor range (at which the 11.5" excels). The shorter barrel makes the gun much easier to handle, and since you definitely won't be using this for shooting past 300 yards (right? RIGHT?) you can put a lightweight red dot or 1x or 2x prism on it rather than a hulking LPVO. If you're naughty, you can configure this as Legally A Pistol and not have to worry about putting yourself on an ATF registry...that is, if you don't plan to suppress it. Which you should. Please suppress this fucking thing, holy shit. Even just a small flow-through K can. I know that gun subculture is disporportionately libertarian, but if you have no other sense of communal responsibility, please exercise it here and buy a fucking can.

As for gas length, I think the simplest answer is "just go carbine length"; the recoil will be sharper and you will have more wear-and-tear on your bolt. But if you are a little nerd who likes to tinker, both KAK and Faxon make 11.5" barrels with mid-length gas intended for suppressed use (though I have read reports of people using them without suppressors with moderate success). And because you intend on suppressing this thing like a good community-minded citizen, you could also get an adjustable gas block and get your 11.5" middy running like a sewing machine. I am considering building an 11.5" middy using some spare parts and whatever I can get on sale this upcoming Black Friday as a dedicated home-defense carbine so that my precious 14.5" never ends up in a police evidence locker until the heat death of the universe.

## A word about ammo: stop putting 77gr OTM in everything!

Every time I read a forum argument about barrel length, I see dudes recommend 77gr open tip match ammo for short barrels, medium barrels, long barrels. Is this weight and projectile design blessed by a team of priests, rabbis, and imams that it is somehow able to defy physics and achieve the correct velocity required for the fragmentation and tumbling that 5.56 is renowned for at any and every distance? No; forum goons just think that retained energy is everything and ignore that heavier bullets out of a shorter barrel don't accelerate enough to retain decent energy at distance, and for point blank defensive applications retain too much momentum to rule out over-penetration.

In reality, there is an inverse relationship between how heavy your bullets should be and the length of your barrel. The shorter your barrel, the less time your projectile has to accelerate, so the lower its top speed will be. And the heavier the bullet, the more energy will be required to make it reach required speeds. Keeping that in mind, you need to calibrate your ammo choice relative to your barrel length, and then twist rate to the resulting bullet weight.

For 14.5", I found that 68gr and 75gr work but I decided to stockpile 68gr because it is slightly cheaper and gets a bit more acceleration than the 75gr.

For 18", congratulations! This is the barrel length for which 77gr OTM was designed! Have fun with the best ammo available in the 5.56 form factor!

For 11.5", I would defer to the FBI ballistics lab and run their precious 64gr jacketed softpoint. Make sure you get both the training version *and* the legit [law enforcement version][fbi-ammo] for home defense.
