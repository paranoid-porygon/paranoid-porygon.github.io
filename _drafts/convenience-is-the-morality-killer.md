Vox published [an article][vox-article] about some internet beef that musician Billie Eilish stirred up by calling a spade a spade regading the dubious (or completely absent) ethics of meat consumption. She received particular consternation from users that appear to be members of the political far left, which should strike any reasonable person as bizarre. Shouldn't the people loudly concerned with inherent rights, deserved regardless of the circumstances of one's birth, be not just tolerant of veganism but its strongest soldiers? 

> [M]any on the left use political slogans to avoid honestly confronting their individual complicity in supporting one of the most violent and environmentally destructive industries on the planet.

The author meant this specifically about meat, but hoo boy do some left-of-center folks, in my experience, hate when you ask them to take a modicum of personal responsibility for their consumption habits, or broader complicity in industries that do material harm.

The line I hear most often from leftists is "there is no ethical consumption under capitalism," which is usually uttered while they make a knowingly stupid and probably unnecessary purchase; it's practically a mantra for them at this point, said under their breath or as an Instagram photo caption of whatever knick-knack made by Uyghur slaves in China they just bought. 

Far-lefties often deny out of hand the idea of *harm reduction*, preferring wholesale solutions even when they themselves take no meaningful action towards any.

This is why I have such respect for anarchists (even though I'm not one): because they prefer a world with no, minimal, or hyperlocal government, *everything* is ultimately either left to personal responsibility or communal obligation and they act like it. That informs their strong ethical commitments and preference for direct action and mutual aid over twiddling their thumbs and hoping the federal/state government will eventually fix a given problem.
