This is a pretty boilerpate fascist talking point --that migrants taint the [blood and soil][3] of a nation-- which is incredibly ironic given the date (Thanksgiving) and the location (the United States) when and where he said it. If there's any truth to his remark, the best evidence for it is the settlement by English Separatist Puritans (aka the Pilgrims) in North America. 

The Puritans were a fundamentalist sect within the Church of England that felt that the Church had not been sufficiently reformed (i.e. stripped of lingering Roman Catholic elements); their movement was largely tolerated by the Church of England until the Puritans began to make a larger stink about liturgical (not even theological) characteristics that they felt were still too Catholic. Growing impatient about the reforms they wanted to see never manifesting, many Puritans separated from the Church of England, gaining the new label of Separatists and inviting criminal prosecution as church attendance was mandated by law. 

There is legitimacy to the claim that Separatists faced persecutionn for their beliefs: some Separatists were executed for their refusal to attend recognized church services, leading to many to **seek refugee in** and **migrate to** the Netherlands, where Calvinist Protestantism like theirs was the majority religion. Over time, England became even less tolerant of Puritan (not even Separatist) teachings and subjected them to censorship and defrocked Purtian ministers. All Puritans were effectively forced to emigrate elsewhere.

A group of Separatists living in exile were able to secure a land grant from the Virginia Company of London to settle New England, which is where the story of The Mayflower, Plymouth, and the Massachusetts Bay Colony begins. 

We need to remember that despite being victims of religious and political persecution, the Puritans, Separatists, and Pilgrims were not by any means a progressive faith or group. Their focus on liturgy as their primary motivation for separation from the Church of England exemplifies that these were religious extremists. Theirs was a theology that prioritized purity of belief and personal piety --things that Jesus himself unambiguously decried in the Gospels-- and enforced those priorities with similar threats of force that the Crown had used against them.
